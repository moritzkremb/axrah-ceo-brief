## Industry & Market

- **Younger consumers are driving adoption.** Per Bon Charge's 2026 Wellness Tech Trend Report, more than half of Americans under 35 have tried or explored red light therapy and related light tech — reinforces that the growth wave is consumer-led, which matters for AXRAH's B2C-adjacent messaging even though the business is B2B-first. ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/more-americans-trying-red-light-235953000.html))
- **Market sizing continues to climb.** Independent estimates now put the global PBM market at roughly $281M (2026) growing to ~$515M by 2033 (10.6% CAGR), with a separate forecast putting it at $0.9B (2025) to $2.4B by 2035 (10.2% CAGR). Directionally consistent with the RLT-beds-specific $8.21B → $19.30B (13% CAGR) figure already in our deck. ([Coherent Market Insights](https://www.coherentmarketinsights.com/industry-reports/photobiomodulation-market), [Vantage Market Research](https://www.vantagemarketresearch.com/photobiomodulation-market))
- **Clinical evidence keeps accumulating on the neuro side.** A University of Toronto (Baycrest/Rotman) team published fMRI evidence that brief pulsed PBM sessions measurably boost cerebrospinal fluid flow — an interesting angle for the longevity/cognitive-health crowd, though it's early-stage brain research, not a product claim AXRAH can use. ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/08/3215395/0/en/University-of-Toronto-Researchers-Report-Real-Time-Brain-Fluid-Modulation-Using-Vielight-Photobiomodulation.html))
- **Dry AMD (eye disease) PBM trial results are mixed.** LIGHTSITE III showed a vision (BCVA) benefit but no change in drusen (the underlying disease marker) — useful nuance if this comes up in sales conversations referencing the earlier FDA PBM/AMD authorization; don't oversell it as a "cure," frame as symptomatic benefit. ([Ophthalmology Advisor](https://www.ophthalmologyadvisor.com/news/photobiomodulation-dry-amd-lightsite-iii-results/))
