## Industry & Market

- **Consumer adoption still climbing:** more than half of Americans under 35 now say they've tried or explored red light therapy; top reported benefits are skin appearance (36%), sleep (29%), and muscle/joint comfort (29%) — reinforces the wellness-mainstream narrative from earlier in the year.
- **Dose-response research is getting more rigorous, and less flattering to "more is better":** multiple independent studies from July–August found the light-dose/effect relationship is non-linear — beyond a threshold, more exposure stops helping and can become counterproductive. This is a useful credibility angle: AXRAH can position its irradiance specs (129 mW/cm² at the Pod Ultra) around "clinically calibrated dosing," not just raw power, ahead of competitors who market on wattage alone.
- **New PBM applications emerging in neuro/cognitive health:** University of Toronto researchers published in *Brain Stimulation* showing pulsed PBM measurably increases cerebrospinal fluid flow via fMRI, and separate coverage flagged a new study on PBM for mild cognitive impairment. Category is expanding credibility beyond skin/recovery into brain health — worth tracking for future positioning, not an immediate action.
- **Market sizing reconfirmed:** photobiomodulation market ~$281M (2026) → $514.8M by 2033 (10.6% CAGR); broader photomedicine market $6.06B (2026) → $10.90B by 2035. Consistent with the RLT-beds $8.21B→$19.30B trajectory already in AXRAH's core numbers.
- **Regulatory tailwind continues:** FDA's 2024 clearance of LumiThera's Valeda system for dry AMD keeps opening the door to light therapy being treated as a first-line clinical intervention, not just wellness. Good ongoing ammunition for medspa/clinical sales conversations.
