## Industry & Market

- Consumer interest keeps climbing: Bon Charge's 2026 Wellness Tech Trend Report has over half of Americans under 35 saying they've tried or explored red light therapy, with top reported benefits being skin appearance (36%), sleep (29%), and muscle/joint comfort (29%). ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/more-americans-trying-red-light-235953000.html))
- Panel-market coverage says the category is "shifting fast" — new wavelengths, higher power outputs, and smarter controls as manufacturers chase both clinical credibility and consumer appeal. This is the exact lane AXRAH's Pod Ultra spec sheet is built to win on. ([Light Therapy Insiders](https://www.lighttherapyinsiders.com/2026-red-light-therapy-panels/))
- Market-size estimates continue to diverge by source (roughly $281M in 2026 → ~$515M by 2033 at 10.6% CAGR on one estimate; a separate estimate puts the broader photomedicine market at $2.4B by 2035) — directionally all point to strong, sustained growth. Treat any single number as approximate in outward-facing materials. ([Coherent Market Insights](https://www.coherentmarketinsights.com/industry-reports/photobiomodulation-market), [Vantage Market Research](https://www.vantagemarketresearch.com/photobiomodulation-market))
- Clinical credibility keeps building outside of pain/recovery: University of Toronto researchers reported pulsed PBM measurably boosts cerebrospinal fluid flow (Jan 2026, published in *Brain Stimulation*), and a March 2026 trial showed multiwavelength PBM improves visual acuity in dry AMD patients. Useful proof points for the "this isn't just wellness fluff" argument with clinical ICPs. ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/08/3215395/0/en/University-of-Toronto-Researchers-Report-Real-Time-Brain-Fluid-Modulation-Using-Vielight-Photobiomodulation.html), [Ophthalmology Advisor](https://www.ophthalmologyadvisor.com/news/photobiomodulation-dry-amd-lightsite-iii-results/))
