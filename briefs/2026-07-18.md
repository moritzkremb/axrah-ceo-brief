## Industry & Market

- **Market growth continues at pace.** Broader RLT/PBM market estimates now cluster around low-double-digit CAGR through the early 2030s (photobiomodulation specifically: ~$281M in 2026 → ~$515M by 2033, ~10.6% CAGR); separate RLT-bed-specific estimates still point toward the $8B→$19B range by 2032. Directionally consistent with prior briefs — the category is not slowing down.
- **Consumer/home-use shift accelerating.** Industry coverage is increasingly framing PBM as moving toward "portable and handheld" and home-use devices, democratizing access outside clinics. This is a double-edged trend for AXRAH: it grows the addressable market and normalizes the category, but also means low-end commoditized devices are multiplying — reinforces the case for staying firmly positioned on clinical-grade/B2B rather than competing on price with panel-only players.
- **Regulatory tailwind continues:** LumiThera's Valeda system remains the first FDA-cleared PBM device for a retinal disease (dry AMD) — still the clearest "PBM is real medicine" data point to use in sales conversations with clinical buyers.
- **New clinical research signal:** University of Toronto researchers published real-time fMRI evidence of PBM measurably changing cerebrospinal fluid dynamics — an interesting, unprompted piece of academic credibility for neuro/wellness applications, though not directly relevant to AXRAH's current product line.
- **Testing rigor becoming a talking point industry-wide.** July coverage (Light Therapy Insiders) highlights growing scrutiny of whether manufacturers' wavelength/power claims hold up to independent measurement. AXRAH should be prepared to substantiate its own specs (43,200 LEDs, 5 wavelengths, 129 mW/cm² on Pod Ultra) with third-party or independently verifiable data if this scrutiny trend reaches B2B buyers — it's a credibility opportunity if AXRAH can pass it and competitors can't.
